# Consulting slide patterns

Read this when the user uses consulting shorthand (tracker, sticker, ghost deck, dot-dash, mekko, Harvey balls and the like), names one of the slide types below, or asks for a section of a deck rather than one slide. Every example here is invented: use the structure, never the numbers.

## Vocabulary

| When the user says | They mean |
| --- | --- |
| Action title, headline | The full-sentence takeaway at the top of the slide. |
| Label title, section title | A short topic in the title box. Templates that use one put the takeaway in a headline box just below it. |
| Tracker | A small label above the title that shows the section or workstream, for example "Diagnostic \| Design \| Pilot" with the current one bold. Used in long decks. |
| Sticker | A small boxed label near the top right that qualifies the whole slide: "Preliminary", "For discussion", "Illustrative", "Draft", "Not exhaustive", "Pre-read". |
| Lede, subtitle | One sentence under the title that tells the reader how to read the evidence. |
| Dot-dash | Two-level bullets: a statement (the dot) with the facts that support it (the dashes) under it. |
| So-what box, call-out, kicker | A box, usually at the bottom or on the right, that states the implication or the action. |
| Source line, footnotes | "Source:" at the bottom of the slide; numbered footnotes for definitions and caveats; "Note:" for how a number was calculated. |
| Ghost deck, skeleton | The storyline as slide titles with placeholder bodies, written before the analysis is finished. |
| Storyline, horizontal logic | The titles in order. Read alone, they tell the story. |
| Pre-read | A deck sent ahead of a meeting. It can be denser than a deck that is presented, because no one is there to explain it. |
| Executive summary | One page with the answer first. Use the executive-summary skill. |
| Backup, appendix | Supporting slides after the main story. |
| One-pager | A single dense page that has to stand alone. |
| Deep dive | A slide or section that unpacks one driver in detail. |
| Issue tree, driver tree, hypothesis tree | A question broken into branches that don't overlap and together cover it. |
| MECE | Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive: no overlaps, no gaps. |
| Waterfall, bridge | Bars that walk a starting value to an end value through its drivers. |
| Marimekko, mekko | A 100% stacked chart whose column widths show size, such as share by segment where the segments differ in size. |
| 2x2 | Two axes, four quadrants, one of them named as where to act. |
| Harvey balls | Quarter-filled circles that rate options on criteria. |
| From-to | Today's state against the target state, matched line by line. |
| SCR | Situation, complication, resolution. |
| Pyramid principle | The answer first, then the grouped arguments that support it. |
| RAG status | Red, amber, green status for initiatives or risks. |

## Slide types

### Performance against plan

Used in monthly or quarterly operating reviews.

- Headline pattern: "[Metric] [above / at / below] plan ([actual] vs [plan]), driven by [driver]". Put the action or outlook in the lede if the title would run past two lines.
- Body: a table of the key metrics (metric, actual, plan, vs plan, prior period), then "What is driving it" as two to four bullets, then "Next steps" with owners.
- Example, invented:
  - **Title:** September occupancy finished 4pp below plan (71% vs 75%), driven by weaker corporate demand at two coastal hotels
  - **Lede:** Leisure bookings held up; the corporate gap is concentrated in mid-week stays.
  - **Body:** **Corporate mid-week:** Room nights down 18% vs plan at the two hotels, after one large account moved its events in-house. **Leisure:** Weekend occupancy at 88%, 2pp above plan. **Rate:** Average daily rate on plan at $212.
  - **Next steps:** Regional sales lead to rebid two corporate accounts by October 15.

### For discussion

Used in workshops, to frame a decision the group has to make.

- Title: "For discussion: [the decision, as a question]?" This is the one place where a question title is right. Elsewhere, state the answer.
- Sticker: "For discussion", or "Preliminary, not exhaustive" when the options are still a draft.
- Body: two to four options as columns or rows: what each one is, its pros, its cons, its rough impact. Mark the option the team proposes, and say why in one line.
- Example, invented:
  - **Title:** For discussion: Should we automate the night shift at the second plant or close it?
  - **Options:** **Automate packing:** $2.1M capex, payback in about 3 years, keeps capacity for peak season. **Close the shift:** Saves about $1.4M a year, but caps output 12% below forecast peak demand. **Keep as is:** No capex, margin keeps eroding as wages rise.
  - **Proposed:** Automate packing, because peak demand is forecast to exceed day-shift capacity from next year.

### Issue tree

Used to structure a problem before the analysis, or to show how the work was organized.

- The question goes at the root, on the left or the top. Two or three levels of branches follow, each level split on one dimension (price and volume; new and existing customers; by region). Each leaf is a hypothesis or an analysis that can be tested.
- The title states what the tree is for, or which branch matters most once the analysis is done.
- Example, invented: "Why did gross margin fall 3pp this year?" splits into **Price** (list price changes, discount depth), **Mix** (product mix, channel mix) and **Cost** (materials, freight, labor). After the analysis, the title becomes: "Discount depth and freight explain most of the 3pp margin decline".

### Approach

Used near the start of a proposal or a project kickoff deck.

- The title states what the approach delivers, or why it fits this problem, not "Our approach".
- Body: three to five phases from left to right, each with its weeks, main activities and deliverable. Optionally, a row for "What we need from you" (data, people, decisions).
- Example, invented: **Title:** A 10-week diagnostic, design and pilot will tell the hospital whether theater scheduling can free 600 hours a quarter. **Phases:** Diagnose (weeks 1 to 3, baseline utilization by specialty), Design (weeks 4 to 6, new scheduling rules with surgical leads), Pilot (weeks 7 to 10, two specialties, weekly tracking).

### Initiatives and impact

Used to show a portfolio of improvement initiatives.

- Rows are initiatives. The columns are a description, the owner, the timing, the estimated impact ($ or %), and confidence or status (Harvey balls, or RAG).
- The title gives the total and where it concentrates: "Eight initiatives are worth about $12M a year, with two pricing moves delivering half of it".
- Mark estimates "est." and add a source line that says how they were sized.

### Benchmark against peers

Used for outside-in diagnostics.

- The title states where the company leads and where it lags: "The bank's cost-income ratio is 6pp above the peer median, driven by branch costs rather than technology spend".
- Body: each metric against the peer median and the top quartile. Name the peer set, the sample and the period in the source line.

### Agenda and trackers

- The agenda lists the sections, as short statements or as questions the deck answers.
- A long deck repeats a tracker on each slide, with the current section highlighted.

### Label and headline templates

Some templates keep a short label in the title box (a section or topic) and put the takeaway in a headline box below it. When the user's template works this way, give both:

**Label:** <section or topic, two to five words>

**Headline:** <the action title>

## Numbers and wording

- Comparisons go in parentheses with "vs": "churn of 2.4% (vs 1.9% plan)".
- Changes in rates are in percentage points, "pp": "+1.2pp".
- Multiples take "x": "pipeline coverage of 3.1x".
- "~" marks a rounded figure; "est." or "(illustrative)" marks an estimate.
- Name the period: FY26, Q3 2026, YTD September, vs prior year.
- Define an abbreviation once, in the lede or a footnote: "ARPU = average revenue per user".
- Bold lead-ins run one to four words. No two lead-ins in the same group start with the same word, and the word after the colon is capitalized.

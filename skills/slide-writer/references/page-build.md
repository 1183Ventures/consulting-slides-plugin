# Building the page

Read this when the user asks how to lay out, build, or format the slide, or when the layout you chose is a comparison table, an evidence stack built on data, a performance-against-plan page, an initiative portfolio, or a plan with steps. Every example here is invented.

## Think of the page as a table

Most strong consulting pages are tables, even when there is no table object on them. Rows are the items you compare (options, regions, initiatives, steps). Columns are what you say about each one (the measures, the criteria, the status). Often the last column, or a panel on the right, holds the so-what. Settle the rows and the columns before you draw anything, and the layout follows.

Two shapes cover most pages:

- **Exhibit and takeaways.** The evidence (a chart or a table) fills the left two-thirds. A panel on the right third holds two or three bold takeaway sentences, each carrying a number that appears in the exhibit. A variant puts the context on the left (the fact that makes this matter) and details the idea on the right.
- **Conceptual table.** The columns are the dimensions of the problem, split so they don't overlap. Each row is one item described along those dimensions. One column is kept for the implication.

## Build tables from text boxes and rules

Many consultants skip the built-in table object for page tables. They build them from separate text boxes, one per cell or one per column, and draw the structure with thin horizontal rules. Each cell then aligns, resizes, and highlights on its own, columns can be spaced to fit their content, and the page has no heavy gridlines.

- **Rules:** a dark rule of about 1.5 pt under the column headers and another above the total row. Between body rows, either a light grey rule of about 0.75 pt or light grey banding on every other row, not both. No vertical lines: white space separates the columns.
- **Group headers:** when several columns share a measure ("Revenue per store, $K, FY25"), put that label above them with a rule under it that spans exactly those columns.
- **Alignment:** text left, numbers right, the same number of decimals down a column. Units and periods go in the header, not in every cell.
- **Total row:** bold, under its dark rule.
- **Highlights:** shade or outline only the cells that carry the title's message, and say what the highlight means in a small legend at the top right.
- **Color:** negative variances may be red. Keep the page to three or four colors in all.

Example, invented: a regional review page. Rows are North, South, East, West, Central, and Total. Columns are Region, Stores, then Actual, Plan, and vs plan under the group header "Revenue per store, $K, FY25", then Growth YoY. The West row's revenue cells are outlined and shaded, and the legend reads "Below plan by more than 10%". The takeaway panel says West is the only region far below plan ($830K per store vs $940K, -12%) and that closing half of that gap adds about $1.7M a year, with a footnote showing the arithmetic.

## Other recurring layouts

- **Grouped plan or checklist:** group labels in dark filled boxes down the left, a bracket from each label to its items, each item a row with a short bold label and one line of detail, and a light rule between rows.
- **Tracker or dashboard:** sections with a bold header and a rule under it, metric rows with the numbers right-aligned, a total row, and the one problem area outlined.
- **Options for discussion:** options as columns, criteria as rows, ratings as Harvey balls or short phrases, and the proposed option boxed.

## Charts

- Time runs left to right as columns. Categories run top to bottom as bars, sorted largest first, with the total at the top and small items grouped into "Other" (footnote what "Other" holds).
- Every chart has a caption with the measure, the unit, and the period ("Revenue per store, $K, FY25").
- Check that the numbers add up after rounding, and footnote any calculation the reader might question.

## Page furniture

- **Title:** two lines at most. If it runs longer, it is saying two things.
- **Sticker:** top right, when the page is not final.
- **Legend:** top right, above the exhibit.
- **Tracker:** above the title in long decks.
- **Source:** bottom left on every page, specific enough to trace (the file, the date, the data set). Numbered footnotes for definitions and calculations.
- **Type:** two font sizes in the body at most. Bold the key words and numbers, not whole sentences.
- **Body area:** keep everything inside the template's body area.

## How to describe the build

When this reference applies, add a **Build:** section to the output, after **Visual** (or after **Layout** when there is no visual). Use three to six short lines:

- the grid: the rows and the columns, in order;
- the rules, banding, and the cells to highlight, with the legend text;
- what goes in the takeaway panel or the so-what column;
- the furniture the page needs: sticker, legend, footnotes, source.

# Slide style

The rules for a page that looks like it came from a strategy consulting team: quiet, aligned, and built so the reader's eye goes straight to the point. Apply them by default. The user's own template and instructions win: if the deck has a master, theme fonts and theme colors, use its placeholders and theme, and apply these rules inside it.

Positions are for a 16:9 page of 13.33 x 7.5 in (1920 x 1080 px). Scale them for other sizes.

| Element | Position | 1920 x 1080 px | 13.33 x 7.5 in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Side margins | 4.5% of the width, both sides | 86 | 0.6 |
| Title, top left | 4.5% across, 2.7% down | 86, 29 | 0.6, 0.2 |
| Title width, full page | 91% | 1745 | 12.1 |

| Body starts | below room for a two-line title | 200 | 1.4 |

## The page

- **Master:** title, body, source line and page number. Nothing else repeats on every page: no small uppercase topic line above the title, unless the deck uses trackers on purpose.
- **Title:** the theme's heading font, bold, about 24 pt, one or two lines, an action title (a full sentence that states the takeaway). Anchor it at the top so a second line grows downward, and start the body below room for two lines.
- **Title width follows the layout,** so it never runs across a side panel.
- **Source line** bottom left, 10 to 12 pt; page number bottom right. Footnotes are superscript numbers, numbered per page, listed at the top of the source block. Nothing else goes in the footer.

## Side panel: the 3/4 page

- Takeaways, implications and "what it means" go in a light grey panel (theme light grey, or #E6E6E6) on the right 28% of the page, full height. The title stops at 65% of the width.
- Panel text is 16 pt, set a little narrower than the panel with equal margins inside the grey, and top-aligned with the body. Stack the panel's blocks so a heading that wraps pushes the text down instead of overlapping it.
- The panel starts at the same height on every page. A chart beside it uses the full width to the left.
- Never float a takeaway box in the middle of the page. Use the panel, or a row inside the page's own structure.

## Tables and lists

Build tables from text boxes and thin rules (or style a table object to look that way): no grid, no cell fills except the highlight.

- **Column headers:** bold, black, no inner margin, at least as large as the text under them (14 pt or more), sitting on one header rule with a small gap above it. The header rule spans every column, including the label column, and every column has a header (Topic, Lead, Phase).
- **Row rules:** thin, centered in the gap between rows, as long as their column's header rule. No rule under the last row. A heavier rule above a total row.
- **Groups:** one rule weight between rows and a clearly different one between groups (for example dotted between items, solid between groups), the same across the deck. When a label spans several rows, the rules inside the group start at the second column, so they never cut through the label; the dividers between groups run the full width.
- **Rules run to the left margin,** under the row labels, so each sits next to what it separates.
- **Row labels and numbers sit flush left,** a list number next to its label ("1  Introductions"), the two centered on one line whatever their sizes.
- **Number columns** are plain, right-aligned numbers, never pills or bubbles.
- **Spacing:** columns spaced evenly across the width, rows spread to fill the page height. A large empty band at the bottom reads as unfinished.
- **Never a wall of bullets.** Short items go in a ruled grid. Two columns that pair up (steps and asks) share one rhythm: the same rows, the same structure, aligned top to top.

## Shapes and diagrams

- **Nothing decorative:** no empty icon squares, circles or check boxes that carry nothing, no 01/02 numbering, no decorative arrows. Use an icon only where it says something, in one style and one color.
- **Labels take the color of what they label,** with a dotted connector to the shape when they sit apart from it.
- **Pyramids and funnels:** names inside the layers. Each layer's boundary continues as a rule across the text beside it, so every row of text lines up with its layer.
- **Chevrons** sit close together with small, even gaps. A timeline starts where its phases start, not at the page edge.
- **An argument reads top to bottom** (situation, complication, resolution) without labeling those words on the page.
- **Options:** compare them as columns in a criteria grid, numbers instead of judgments wherever a number exists, Harvey balls with a one-row key, and the recommended option marked with a dashed band.

## Charts

- **Exhibit title:** a bold title with a grey qualifier that gives the unit and period: "Sales and costs by month ($K, Jan to Aug 2026)".
- **Clean:** direct labels instead of axes where possible, no frame, no heavy gridlines, a light baseline, a little smaller than the space allows.
- **The chart fits the message:** growth as stacked columns with a growth-rate arrow, a bridge as a waterfall, size and share as a Mekko, a plan as a Gantt, ranges as a football field.
- **Beside a table,** a bar chart gets thin rules centered between the bars, running under the category labels like table rows.
- **Small multiples** share one scale, and each gets its own short header rule.

## Legends and color

- **Every color or shading means something and has a legend.** The legend is one row at the top right of the exhibit, right-aligned to the content edge, just above the column headers: each marker left of its label, labels about 12 pt in grey, an even gap between items. No lead-in such as "Status:": put the meaning in the label ("High risk"). Use the same wording for the same shading on every page.
- **Colors come from the theme** (theme slots, so a client's theme recolors the page). If the deck has none, use navy #0F1B2D, blue #2C6CB0, light blues #5B8FD0 and #9DBFE3, grey #8A96A8, light grey #E6E6E6, and gold #C4A35A as the single highlight.
- **One highlight per page,** on what the title talks about. Red, amber and green only for status.

## Type and text

- Body 14 pt, insight panels 16 pt, sources and footnotes 10 to 12 pt. Headers never smaller than the text under them.
- Bullets open with a bold lead-in that ends in a colon; the text after it starts with a capital letter.
- Money: "$" with a capital K, M or B; negatives in parentheses ("($43K) operating loss").
- No filler lines that repeat what a chart already shows. Prefer colons and commas to dashes.

## Before you hand it over

Check the page against these rules, then run the slide review skill if it is installed: a number in the title is on the page, every chart has a unit and a period, a page with figures has a source line, and table totals add up.
